# English writing mode

Use this reference only when the requested output language is English. Project constraints, subject-matter safety, factual fidelity and the writer's sample remain more important than these suggestions.

- Lead with the answer, evidence or concrete action. Do not open with "Let's dive in", "Great question", "Here's the thing" or an apology unless they have a real purpose.
- Replace inflated abstractions ("transformative journey", "pivotal moment", "seamless ecosystem") with verified details.
- Avoid automatic contrast frames ("not just X, but Y"), dramatic one-line closers, fixed triads and repeated sentence openings when they are decorative rather than informative.
- Use active subjects and plain verbs where it improves precision. Do not erase responsibility or causal nuance.
- Prefer factual attribution to "experts say" or "studies show" unless the source can actually be named.
- Keep genuine uncertainty. "May", "could", "in this sample" and "not statistically significant" may be essential scientific meaning, not hedging to remove.
- Keep useful lists, technical labels, code blocks and references. Strip empty formatting only when it obstructs the reader.
- Match the author's deliberate stylistic features, including contractions, punctuation and humor. Never invent autobiographical details, quotations, statistics or sources.
- A final rewrite must not add or drop factual claims, ranks, negations, time relations, percentages, sample sizes, exclusions or limitations.

For scientific, legal and personal material, additionally read `references/sensitive-text.md`. For broad fidelity checks, use `references/fidelity.md`.
